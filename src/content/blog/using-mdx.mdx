    <h1>
      Pakistan’s Auto Sector Accelerates EV Shift Amid Rising Fuel Costs and Regional Uncertainty
    </h1>

    <div class="meta">
      Published on May 10, 2026 • EV Industry • Pakistan Auto Market
    </div>

    <p>
      Pakistan’s automobile industry is witnessing renewed momentum in electric and hybrid mobility as rising fuel prices and regional instability reshape consumer behavior. Local assemblers and investors are moving aggressively toward electric bikes, hybrid SUVs, and compact EVs.
    </p>

    <p>
      The latest developments involve possible electric bike production by one of Pakistan’s largest tractor manufacturers, alongside new hybrid and electric vehicle plans from Chery Master Pakistan and fresh expansion activity across the two-wheeler segment.
    </p>

    <h2>Electric Bike Demand Surges Across Pakistan</h2>

    <img 
      class="section-image"
      src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1600712242805-5f78671b24da?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop"
      alt="Electric scooter"
    />

    <p>
      Electric bikes and scooters are seeing a sharp increase in demand following continuous hikes in petrol prices. Consumers in major cities are increasingly shifting toward EV alternatives due to lower running costs and reduced dependence on fuel.
    </p>

    <p>
      Industry observers report that electric bikes and scooters experienced strong nationwide sales during March and April, with growing interest particularly in urban centers where daily commuting costs have become a major concern.
    </p>

    <div class="highlight">
      Shortages of some popular electric bike models have reportedly triggered “on money” premiums ranging from Rs10,000 to Rs15,000 in several markets across Pakistan.
    </div>

    <h3>Import Activity Increases as Assemblers Respond</h3>

    <p>
      To address supply shortages, electric bike assemblers have accelerated imports of parts and accessories by opening additional letters of credit over recent weeks.
    </p>

    <p>
      Manufacturers appear to expect EV demand to remain strong in the short term, especially if fuel prices continue rising. The recent surge has also significantly improved public awareness and acceptance of electric mobility.
    </p>

    <h2>Millat Group Exploring Electric Bike Production</h2>

    <img 
      class="section-image"
      src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1489824904134-891ab64532f1?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop"
      alt="Electric motorcycle"
    />

    <p>
      Reports recently emerged suggesting that Millat Group could enter Pakistan’s electric bike sector. In a stock market filing, the company clarified that one of its group companies is evaluating the feasibility of producing electric bikes locally.
    </p>

    <p>
      Discussions remain at an early stage, but the move could become significant due to the company’s large-scale manufacturing experience and established vendor network.
    </p>

    <h3>Why Millat’s Entry Could Matter</h3>

    <ul>
      <li>Strong manufacturing infrastructure</li>
      <li>Existing nationwide dealer network</li>
      <li>Experience in vehicle assembly</li>
      <li>Potential for localized production</li>
      <li>Possibility of lower EV prices in the future</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Chery Master Pakistan Plans New Hybrid and EV Models</h2>

    <img 
      class="section-image"
      src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1617788138017-80ad40651399?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop"
      alt="Electric car"
    />

    <p>
      Chery Master Pakistan is also expanding its ambitions in Pakistan’s evolving mobility market. Following participation at Auto China 2026, the company is evaluating the introduction of several new vehicles for Pakistani consumers.
    </p>

    <p>
      The possible upcoming lineup includes:
    </p>

    <ul>
      <li>Tiggo 4 HEV</li>
      <li>QQ BEV</li>
      <li>Tiggo V</li>
    </ul>

    <h3>Compact EVs Could Target Pakistani Cities</h3>

    <p>
      Among the most notable vehicles under consideration is the QQ BEV, a compact electric vehicle designed mainly for urban transportation.
    </p>

    <div class="highlight">
      The QQ BEV reportedly offers a driving range of up to 410 km, making it attractive for daily commuting in congested cities such as Karachi, Lahore, and Islamabad.
    </div>

    <h2>Pakistan’s Hybrid SUV Segment Continues to Expand</h2>

    <p>
      Chery Master Pakistan has already introduced multiple locally assembled plug-in hybrid SUVs into the market, including the Tiggo 9 PHEV, Tiggo 8 PHEV, and Tiggo 7 PHEV.
    </p>

    <p>
      Hybrid and plug-in hybrid vehicles are becoming increasingly attractive in Pakistan because they offer lower fuel consumption without relying entirely on charging infrastructure.
    </p>

    <h3>Why Hybrid Vehicles Are Gaining Popularity</h3>

    <ul>
      <li>Lower fuel consumption</li>
      <li>Reduced dependence on petrol</li>
      <li>No major range anxiety issues</li>
      <li>Growing awareness of EV technology</li>
      <li>Increasing urban commuting costs</li>
    </ul>

    <h2>Challenges Facing Pakistan’s EV Industry</h2>

    <img 
      class="section-image"
      src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1593941707882-a5bba53b3f87?q=80&w=1200&auto=format&fit=crop"
      alt="EV charging"
    />

    <h3>Charging Infrastructure Remains Limited</h3>

    <p>
      Public charging stations remain limited outside major urban centers, creating concerns for long-distance electric vehicle travel.
    </p>

    <h3>Heavy Dependence on Imports</h3>

    <p>
      Most EV assemblers continue to rely heavily on imported batteries, motors, and electronic components, exposing the industry to currency fluctuations and import restrictions.
    </p>

    <h3>Need for Long-Term Policy Stability</h3>

    <p>
      Industry growth will also depend on consistent government incentives, taxation policies, and long-term infrastructure planning.
    </p>

    <h2>The Bigger Picture</h2>

    <p>
      Pakistan’s automotive market appears to be entering a major transition phase where electric and hybrid mobility is moving from niche interest toward mainstream adoption.
    </p>

    <p>
      Rising fuel prices, urban congestion, and changing consumer preferences are pushing manufacturers to diversify beyond conventional petrol-powered vehicles.
    </p>

    <p>
      While the industry still faces infrastructure and policy challenges, recent developments suggest Pakistan’s EV adoption curve may accelerate much faster than previously expected.
    </p>
